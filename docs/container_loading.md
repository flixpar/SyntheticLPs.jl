# Container Loading

Loading and two-dimensional packing LPs. Both variants store a typed witness
for `feasible` requests and a relaxation-valid certificate that aggregates
many rows for `infeasible` ones; `unknown` is natural and comes out both ways.

## `standard` (default) — heterogeneous ISO container fleet

Palletised consignments are loaded into a fleet of ISO containers under three
limits: payload (kg), volume (m³) and floor (pallet) positions.

- **Fleet.** Containers drawn from `CONTAINER_TYPES` — 20 ft (28.2 t,
  33.2 m³, 11 pallets), 40 ft (26.7 t, 67.7 m³, 24), 40 ft HC (26.5 t,
  76.4 m³, 24), 45 ft HC (27.7 t, 86 m³, 27), 40 ft reefer (27.4 t, 59.3 m³,
  23) with mix 25/30/25/10/10% and relative costs 1.00/1.45/1.55/1.75/2.10.
  A 20 ft box carries as much weight as a 45 ft high-cube but a third of the
  volume, so weight-critical and volume-critical consignments prefer
  different boxes.
- **Consignments.** 1-6 pallets of 1.1-1.9 m³ each; density from a light/bulky
  (lognormal around 140 kg/m³) vs dense (around 520 kg/m³) mixture. Weight
  and volume are normalised to 70-82% of fleet capacity, pallets to at most 75%.

```text
min  sum_b cost_b used_b
s.t. sum_b assign_ib = 1                       (every consignment)
     assign_ib <= used_b                       (every pair; strong linking)
     sum_i req_id assign_ib <= cap_bd used_b   (every container, 3 dimensions)
```

Sizing: `n_items * n_containers + n_containers` columns, choosing the closest
fit with 4-8 consignments per container slot (`container_loading_dimensions`;
within 1-2% of the target). Rows `n + n b + 3 b`. The previous search always
picked 2 containers: every assignment row was a doubleton that presolve used
to eliminate half the columns, and 50k instances were 25,000 × 2 degenerate
LPs that hit a 60 s limit.

- `feasible`: first-fit-decreasing loading (consignments shrunk by 8% steps
  until it fits) — `ContainerLoadingWitness`.
- `infeasible`: total weight raised to `U(1.04, 1.12)` × fleet payload —
  `ContainerOverloadCertificate` (every assignment row plus every payload row).
- `unknown`: weight and volume utilisation drawn independently in
  `U(0.86, 1.01)`; whether consignments can be split so every box respects all
  three limits at once is up to the LP.

## `two_dimensional_bin_packing` — two-stage guillotine patterns

Rectangular items (integer mm, no rotation) are cut from sheets — first into
horizontal strips, then across each strip (Gilmore & Gomory 1965). Sheets:
1-3 types from `3210 × 2250`, `2800 × 2070`, `2440 × 1220` mm (glass, board,
plywood) with area-proportional costs. Item heights come from a catalogue of
standard heights; the distinct heights are the strip classes.

```text
min  sum_q cost_s(q) z_q
s.t. sum_p a_ip y_p >= d_i                              (item demand)
     sum_{p in (c,s)} y_p - sum_{q of s} b_qc z_q <= 0   (strip balance per class, sheet type)
     sum_{q of s} z_q <= A_s                             (sheet availability)
```

Strip patterns `y_p` (items across a strip of class `c` on sheet type `s`)
and sheet patterns `z_q` (strips stacked in height) are generated by a
hash-deduplicated greedy enumerator; the balance rows couple the two levels.
Columns: exactly the target (85% strip, 15% sheet patterns; minimum 4).
`n_types = round(n / 20)`, classes `clamp(round(1.5 sqrt(n_types)), 2, 80)`.

This replaces the pairwise big-M left/right/above/below disjunction model,
whose LP relaxation collapsed to `max(1, total area / bin area)` (every
non-overlap row slack at the fractional point) and had `O(n²)` variables.

- `feasible`: single-item strips and single-class sheets
  (`TwoStagePlanWitness`), availability `U(1.05, 1.35)` × plan sheets.
- `infeasible`: availability scaled so item area exceeds sheet area by
  `U(8%, 20%)` (`AreaShortageCertificate`; multipliers `w_i h_i`, `W_s H_c`,
  `W_s H_s`).
- `unknown`: sheet area `U(0.98, 1.18)` × item area.
